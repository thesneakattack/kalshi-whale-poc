phase 113 · the whole exchange, and the number it settles on

Exchange-wide trade stream, the CF Benchmarks settlement feed, and the measurement that decides whether it's an edge

Started from a direct question — "if whales are winning at 70% I shouldn't be winning at 40" — and the answer needed a tool that didn't exist. services/series_watcher.py reconciles the two numbers per series and decomposes the gap: on KXBTC15M over 24h, whales resolved 88.8% correct while the realised win rate was 47.1%, of which −30.6pts is selection (the 12 signals the gates actually traded resolved only 58.3% correct against an 88.8% pool — the gates pick worse than the pool they draw from) and −11.2pts is exits. It also captures what the app had been discarding: _process_stream_ticker received 15 documented ticker fields and kept two, dropping yes_bid_size_fp, open_interest_fp and ts_ms — exactly the fields needed to ask why a directionally-correct signal still lost money. Raw payloads are now persisted whole, raw_json included, so a field Kalshi adds next month is captured the day it appears.

Coverage was the other half. docs/kalshi/public-trades.md states market specification is optional on the trade channel, so it now subscribes with no market_tickers at all — push, not poll, zero REST tokens, and it closes a gap where 425 markets traded in a 30-second window against 15 watched. Three things had to change for that to produce signals rather than noise: the provider used to skip any print whose ticker wasn't in the watchlist one line into scoring, so the notional gate now runs first (pure arithmetic, no I/O) and only survivors get a market resolved — 2 of 5,162 prints on KXBTC15M, so the fetch is paid for the 2; kalshi_trade_ws tracked trade and ticker with one shared flag, which would have duplicated or blocked the firehose; and per-message SQLite writes would have re-triggered the 2026-08-11 freeze, so captures batch.

The largest find came from reading the settlement rules instead of the prices. KXBTC15M resolves on "the simple average of the sixty seconds of CF Benchmarks' BRTI before close", with floor_strike carrying the previous quarter's average — already fixed and known. Kalshi streams that exact average as it accumulates (cfbenchmarks_value's last_60s_windowed_average_15min), so services/index_feed.py is not a signal about the outcome but partial knowledge of it, and required_remaining_average() inverts it exactly. Verified against a real 06:00:00Z boundary: the window opened at 05:59:00 and counted 3 → 58 of 60 second-by-second, with 31 of the 60 settlement observations known 29 seconds before close.

Nothing about it was assumed. A sweep of eight crypto series found they contradict each other — strike_type is greater_or_equal on the 15-minute series but greater on the dailies; the same index is named BRTI, ETHUSDRTI and ERTI across different rules; KXBTCMAXY passes both the strike and operator checks but is a year-long barrier market with no averaging window; KXDOGED has no strike at all — so settlement_spec() refuses rather than guesses on each. Wiring it also exposed a real starvation bug: subscribed alone the channel delivers exactly 50 messages in 50 seconds, but multiplexed behind the trade firehose it delivered ~20 and froze, because the single reader loop awaited each message's full handler before reading the next. run() now splits ingest from processing over a bounded queue with counted overflow, and the index feed gets its own connection.

Whether any of that is an edge is a separate question, so services/settlement_edge.py answers it by measurement instead of assertion: both forecasts of the same event — the market's own yes price and the projection — recorded at the same instant, Brier-scored once the outcome stamps onto those same rows. It reports insufficient rather than a verdict until the data earns one, and nothing trades on it. Inspecting its captured rows immediately found two bugs that the wiring had hidden: the q15 window opens before every quarter-hour, so matching on index alone had recorded 1,003 observations against KXBTCD markets whose close was 863 minutes away; and resolution driven from the trading loop's market list never fired, because the 15-minute series rotates its ticker out of the watchlist before result populates — a market was found finalized six minutes after close with 59 observations still pending. Both fixed and verified live: bad rows purged, 59/59 resolved.

Two supporting pieces landed alongside. services/trade_archive.py copies every trade, position and summary metric into an append-only epoch before POST /api/reset touches anything, because a prior reset had left paper_broker.db reaching back only six hours and made every earlier trade-level question unanswerable; its compare() ranks epochs by edge_pts, not win rate. And min_unit_cost moved 0.50 → 0.65 on measurement, not taste: across 593 resolved signals the 0.50–0.65 band runs −35.2% per dollar risked while 0.65–0.80 is the only positive window at +2.2%. 1,084 tests passing.
